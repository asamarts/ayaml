## Why

Two bounded resource-hygiene improvements surfaced during the pre-1.0 audit are safe to defer
to `1.0.x` (they are bounded by `max_input_bytes` / `MAX_DEPTH_CAP` today) while remaining
worth fixing for production defense in depth:

1. **Eager pre-limit scalar and emitter allocations:** `Scanner::make` checks `max_scalar_bytes`
   only after `scan_double_quoted`, `scan_single_quoted`, and `scan_block_scalar` decode the
   full scalar, allowing temporary 2x to 3x allocations before rejecting inputs under tight
   `Limits::strict()`. Likewise, `double_quoted`, `expand_folded`, `TagDirectives::lines`, and
   `scalar_inline` allocate complete escaped/folded strings before `Output::push_str` checks
   `max_output_bytes`, and `event_emitter.rs` validates directives against `Limits::default()`
   rather than caller output limits.
2. **Native stack recursion in `ValueWriter` and `TagDirectives::scan_at`:** Unlike `Parser`
   and `EventWriter` (which use explicit heap stacks), `ValueWriter` and `TagDirectives::scan_at`
   recurse on the native thread stack up to `limits.max_depth`. Converting them to explicit
   heap stacks eliminates stack-size sensitivity when callers configure high custom depths.
3. **Minor redundant scan in `scanner/scalars.rs`:** `line_end` is computed twice identically
   inside the `leading < probe` loop.

## Scope

1. Pass `max_scalar_bytes` into `scan_double_quoted`, `scan_single_quoted`, and
   `scan_block_scalar` and fail immediately as soon as decoded length exceeds the limit.
2. Stream escaped and folded scalar emission directly through `Output` (checking
   `max_output_bytes` incrementally) and thread caller output limits into directive validation
   in `event_emitter.rs`.
3. Replace native recursion in `ValueWriter` (`emitter.rs`) and `TagDirectives::scan_at`
   (`emitter/tags.rs`) with explicit heap work stacks.
4. Remove the redundant `line_end` scan in `scanner/scalars.rs` and add unit/adversarial tests
   confirming tight-limit rejection with minimal peak heap and high-depth emission without
   stack overflow.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] oversized quoted/block scalars fail incrementally without allocating full decoded buffers beyond `max_scalar_bytes`
- [ ] `ValueWriter` and `EventWriter` enforce `max_output_bytes` incrementally without full escaped temporary allocations
- [ ] `ValueWriter` and `TagDirectives::scan_at` use explicit heap stacks and pass deep-nesting tests on small thread stacks
- [ ] full workspace tests, allocation budgets, and Callgrind instruction gates pass cleanly
